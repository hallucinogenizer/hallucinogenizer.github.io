The verdict

On the totals this was a good week. On the two lines that have moved this year it was an ordinary one, and ordinary now costs about 11.4 lakh a year more than it did — of which about half is the petrol pump, a sick sister-in-law and a cook you hired to feed her household.

Before anything else: none of what follows is about your giving. The qarz, the stipends, your father’s bills, the donations — those are decisions you have made and restated, and I am treating them as fixed. This report is about the money that leaves without a decision attached to it.

One. The headline number is genuinely good. 161,975 of booked expense is 48% below your trailing twelve-week mean of 310,497 and the lowest since the week of 22 June. There was no technology, no travel and no social spending at all. On its own that is a well-run week.

Two. It hid the only two things worth finding. Counting food in every form — Food category, groceries, the chef, the split parents that are meals — this week cost 49,467 against a Mar–Aug 2025 average of 16,807. That is not a one-week spike: the past six months average 31,111 a week against 16,807 in the same six months of 2025 — up 85%. Transport is 17,072 against 9,480, up 80%. Together those two lines are worth 1,138,608 a year more than they were, and your Transportation category still reads below average this week, because the average it is measured against is a year old.

Three. Two things you did this week were new and right. You did not touch Al Meezan once in seven days without income — you let Meezan Bank fall from 321,325 to 98,212 and left the fund at 240,185. And you opened the voluntary pension account on 1 September, which closes the largest structural gap in your FY26 position.

The line that matters. Your raise from $66,000 to $80,000 is worth about 3,794,000 a year. Food and transport have grown by 1,138,608 against the same six months last year — 30% of the raise. 558,168 of that now has a name: the pump, the coffee, Anila, the daily run to Harbanspura, and one borrowed car. The remaining 580,440 does not, and almost all of it sits in one place.

Method

Week defined as Monday 31 August to Sunday 6 September 2026 inclusive, on transaction date. Source is the 7 September export, 8,957 rows, of which 81 fall in the window. Balances are reconstructed from a zero start in December 2021 using the app’s own transfer semantics, which reproduce its displayed balances.

Booked spending is the sum of rows typed Expense. True outlay adds the net growth in the three ledgers that function as giving rather than lending — Abujan 48,797, Tabish 15,000, Mama 5,000 — using the change in balance rather than the gross funding, so the funding and the spending are not counted twice. Transfers to Bhatti, Wasiq, Fahad, Office Loans and Split are excluded because they are claims, not spending. Spendable cash is Meezan plus physical cash plus NayaPay plus Saving Pot, net of the card. Free liquid is all fourteen liquid accounts net of the card and of the 7,084 still owed to Umaid; it includes the 31,049 stranded in Wise, which is 382,066 without it.

All food is gross cash out: expense rows in Food, in Household / General (groceries) and the Family rows whose notes name the chef or a meal, plus the Split parent rows whose notes are meals or coffee. Rows sourced from Split are excluded so that a parent and its legs are never both counted. Transport is Transportation / Car and Transportation / Taxi, which keeps food delivered by InDrive out of the transport figure.

All trend comparisons use 1 March to 31 August, 2026 against 2025 — six months against the same six months, both sides computed with identical definitions. That window was chosen after testing: an eight-week cut of the same data put the transport change at +153% where six months puts it at +80%, because the eight weeks happened to compare a quiet stretch of 2025 against a busy one of 2026. Where a shorter window is used it is labelled and it is used for counts, not rates. Fuel is converted to litres using the OGRA MS-92 price series, monthly, as a proxy for hi-octane, which is unregulated and unpublished.

Comparatives are the FY26 figures: 13,527,753 of personal spend, 14,747,871 including qarz treated as gifts, and a trailing twelve-week mean of booked expense from 8 June to 30 August of 310,497. Six Split clusters totalling 19,717 were unallocated at the time of writing, so booked spending is provisional to that extent. The usual caveat applies on top: unrecorded transactions get trued up later as do hisaab plugs at roughly 31,000 a month, so read any single week as plus or minus about seven thousand.
